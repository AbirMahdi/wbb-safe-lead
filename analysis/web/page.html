The safety map

A lead's worth grows as the clock runs

Each square is one game situation: a lead size at a minute mark. Its shade shows how often teams in that spot went on to win, on the 50% to 100% scale above the chart. A 10-point lead at halftime held 86% of the time. With 2 minutes left, the same 10 points held 99.9% of the time.

The orange line marks the lead that wins 95 times in 100 between evenly matched teams. Above the line you can start to relax.

The rule of thumb

The square-root rule

The safe line follows a simple curve. In an even matchup, a lead is about 95% safe once it reaches

safe lead ≈ 3.7 × √(minutes left)

Time does not shrink a comeback's odds in a straight line. The number of possessions left falls with the clock, but the swings a team can string together shrink more slowly, roughly with the square root. That is why a 9-point lead with 5 minutes left is about as safe as a 17-point lead at halftime.

Who's leading matters

Early leads tell you who is better. Late leads tell you who will win.

A 10-point lead in the first quarter means very different things depending on who has it. When a big favorite leads by 10, it usually stays that way, because the better team built the lead. When an underdog leads by 10 early, the favorite comes back far more often.

By the fourth quarter the lines come together. With little time left, the score matters more than the talent gap.

When the math was wrong

Four comebacks, play by play

A 1% chance is not zero. In these four seasons, 89 teams won a game after the model gave them less than a 1% chance, about one a week during the season. Pick a game to replay it: the top chart is the score margin, the bottom is the comeback team's chance to win at each moment.

The Big Ten

Who closes, and who comes back

Since the Big Ten grew to 18 teams in 2024-25, each team has had dozens of chances to protect a double-digit lead and dozens of chances to erase one. The chart puts both on one map.

UCLA almost never trailed by 10 and never lost a 10-point lead. Purdue protected 10-point leads about as well as the conference middle, but turned only one of 33 double-digit deficits into a win.

Boiler up

Purdue's best comeback in this data came in the 2023 Big Ten Tournament. Down 18 to Wisconsin, with a 12% chance to win by the model's count, the Boilermakers rallied. Jayla Smith's three with 7 seconds left won it, 57-55. It is the fifth-largest comeback in a game between two Big Ten teams over these four seasons. Replay it above.

How this was made

Methods and sources

Data

Play-by-play, team box scores and schedules from the 2027 Love Data Week NCAA Women's Basketball data set (Big Ten Academic Alliance), sourced from ESPN and processed by the SportsDataverse wehoop project. Four seasons, 2022-23 through 2025-26, regular season and postseason.

Two supplements come from the public wehoop release of the same plays. First, ESPN's pregame win probability, which the BTAA file does not include. Second, corrected running scores: ESPN's raw feed, which the BTAA file carries, sometimes logs free throws out of order, so the score flickers by a point or two. That affects 17,678 of 8.6 million plays and creates comebacks that never happened. Oklahoma's 100-46 win over Coppin State, for example, would show a 28-point comeback, though Coppin State never led. Apart from those scores, every play, game and final score matches the BTAA file exactly.

Cleaning

  • Kept games marked complete whose play-by-play has all four quarters, more than 200 plays, and a final score matching the official result: 22,707 of the 23,400 completed games in the official data set (97%). Every kept game matches the official final score and teams.
  • Read the score at every whole minute of regulation. "Held" means the team ahead at that moment won the game, overtime included.
  • Checked comebacks against ESPN's box-score "largest lead" field. All the comebacks named on this page agree. In 31 games (0.1%), the play-by-play and box score differ by a few points on the largest lead; none appears on this page, and none falls in the Big Ten chart's seasons.

The model

  • Fit by logistic regression on minute-by-minute game states from 2022-23 to 2024-25. Tested on held-out games from 2025-26.
  • On the test season, the model's Brier score was , compared with for the pregame forecast alone (lower is better). When it said 85%, teams won 86% of the time.
  • About 2,250 games had no real pregame forecast (ESPN's placeholder value of 58.6%). They count in "All games" but are left out of the model and the matchup groups.

Limits

  • These are patterns, not causes. A team's comeback rate reflects its overall strength as much as anything it does late in games.
  • Team-level counts are small. Treat differences of a few percentage points between Big Ten teams as noise.
  • The model ignores possession, fouls and timeouts. Under 2 minutes, those matter.
